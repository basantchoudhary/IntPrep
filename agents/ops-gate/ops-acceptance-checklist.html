Ops Gate

Ops Acceptance Checklist — Data Engineering Projects

Phase 1 · plain English · 170 items across 16 sections
Read every item from the receiving side

"Has the project team done this, and is the evidence in our hands?" — not as work for Ops to do. Ops is not closing the gaps here. Ops is establishing whether the gaps exist, and declining to inherit a system whose gaps are unknown.

"They said so in a meeting" is not evidence. Every row names the artefact that must physically be handed over or pointed at.

What this document deliberately does not contain

No tooling, no products, no automation. How each item gets verified — by a rule, by an agent assembling material for a human, or by a named signature — is phase 2, and doing it now would let the tooling decide the bar.

The bar comes first. What can be automated is discovered afterwards, and the share that can be is the maturity measure.

Notes

Working notes & what happens next

How to use the list, and phase 2
On the severity split — this needs a second pass

As graded: 89 Blocker · 65 Major · 16 Minor. Over half the list blocking is a tier-1 reading, and if every profile inherits it, the word stops meaning anything.

The next edit should be a deliberate down-grading pass per tier, targeting roughly 30–40 blockers for a mid-tier system. Grading severity per profile rather than globally is probably the right end state.

On the shape of the list

170 items is far too many to walk through in a meeting — and that is fine, because it is not a meeting agenda. It is the superset from which a per-project profile is drawn.

A tier-1 regulated pipeline uses nearly all of it. An internal reporting job uses a fraction. The profile is selected by the criticality tier agreed in OWN-09, which is why that item sits in the first section.

The items that will be contested

Expect pushback on RES-06 (restore actually tested), REL-05 (rollback demonstrated), PRF-03 (tested at production volume), and ACC-02 / ACC-03 (Ops runs it themselves).

All five are contested for the same reason: they require doing the thing rather than describing it. That is precisely why they are on the list, and why conceding them hollows out the rest.

On rejecting a handover

The point of a written bar is that "no" stops being a personality trait and becomes a reading of a list. The conversation moves from judgement to evidence, and the project team can see the bar before they hit it.

Every blocker should be defensible as "this is what we would need at 3am". An item that cannot be defended that way is a Major, not a Blocker.

Phase 2 — turning this into a gate (not started)

ClassMeaning
AutoA rule over evidence a machine can collect. The verdict is deterministic and reproducible in three years.
AssembledAn agent gathers and cross-references the material; a human makes the call. Never auto-passed.
AttestedCannot be automated. A named person signs, dated, with an expiry.
WaivedExplicitly not applicable — with a reason, an approver and an expiry. A blank is not a waiver.
The share of items in Auto is the maturity number, and it is designed to rise release on release. One rule carries over from the implementation and should govern the classification work: nothing passes by absence. An item that cannot be verified is not an item that passed.
